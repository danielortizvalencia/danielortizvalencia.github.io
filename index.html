Research

Robot Learning TicketBank: location-targeted noise search for frozen diffusion policies
Daniel Ortiz Valencia
Under review, 2026

A frozen diffusion policy reuses one noise sample everywhere. Does the best one depend on where the objects start? Pre-registered gains of 25.8 and 20.8 points on two Franka checkpoints, nothing significant on the other two, and on Transport the early advantage did not survive compute matching.

Robot Learning Physical persistence of sampled noise in diffusion policies
Daniel Ortiz Valencia
In progress, 2026

Does sampled noise leave a lasting physical signature? Across 6,176 validated episodes, start state plus noise predicts the 100-step tool path 30.96% better than start state alone. The effect fades: by 300 actions the model is worse than state alone.

Perception Incremental LiDAR-Camera Sensor Fusion for Reactive Cone Following in the Formula Student Driverless Simulator
Daniel Ortiz Valencia, RAM Racing Driverless
Formula Student Driverless Simulator, 2026
Website/ PDF/ arXiv soon

A LiDAR-only controller completes laps but weaves on every straight, because it cannot tell blue cones from yellow. Fusing a YOLOv5 detector with LiDAR depth brings both sensors into agreement at 6 cm and removes the oscillation.

Reinforcement Learning Reinforcement Learning for Steering Control in the Formula Student Driverless Simulator: Structural Failure Modes of Soft Actor-Critic and the Case for PPO
Daniel Ortiz Valencia
Electrical & Computer Engineering, Colorado State University, 2026
Website/ PDF

Six training runs of Soft Actor-Critic, all six collapsed. The failures trace to three structural properties of the algorithm rather than to tuning, and PPO removes all three by construction.

Safety / ML YOLO Color Anomaly Detection for FSAE Driverless: From Ground-Truth Dataset Construction to an XGBoost Safety Gate
Daniel Ortiz Valencia
ECE553, Colorado State University, 2026
PDF/ code soon

YOLO fails silently, returning a confident colour label that is wrong. I labelled 129,025 detections to measure how often, then trained an XGBoost gate that catches about 91% of those errors at under 0.3 ms per frame.
