I am a final-year Computer Engineering undergraduate at Colorado State University, applying to graduate programs in robotics and machine learning. I work on autonomy: how a robot turns raw sensing into a decision, and what happens when the learned part of that pipeline fails.

Most of my research so far is on the Formula Student Driverless Simulator. I built the perception stack that fuses LiDAR with a camera to follow a cone-delimited track, then tried to replace the hand-written controller with a learned policy and documented the ways that went wrong. I also contribute to robot teleoperation work, on a WebXR system that drives a bimanual arm through differential inverse kinematics.

Before the research, I founded Colorado State's first Formula SAE Electric program and designed its 324V battery system. I started undergraduate physics at Universidad de Nariño at 16 before moving to the United States to continue in engineering.

Robot Learning TicketBank: location-targeted noise search for frozen diffusion policies
Daniel Ortiz Valencia
Under review, 2026

A frozen diffusion or flow-matching policy is normally run with one fixed noise sample reused everywhere, a "golden ticket". I test whether the best sample actually depends on where the objects start, and whether a bank of samples chosen per start state beats a single one at an equal episode budget. On a Franka checkpoint the pre-registered bank wins by 25.8 and 20.8 points on two of four checkpoints and ties on the rest. On robomimic Can at scale, 68,000 episodes, it beats a shuffled-bank control by 3.8 points but not the best single ticket. On Transport the early advantage did not survive compute matching: given the same budget the baseline search reaches 59.7% against the bank's 37.8%, which retired that claim. Every confirmatory run is pre-registered with a frozen script and fresh seeds, and nulls are reported alongside the wins.

Robot Learning Physical persistence of sampled noise in diffusion policies
Daniel Ortiz Valencia
In progress, 2026

Does the noise a diffusion policy samples leave a lasting physical signature, and can you tell what a trajectory will do before executing it? In a frozen study of 6,176 validated episodes, a ridge model given the start state plus the noise predicts the 100-step tool path with 30.96% lower median error than start state alone, with a 95% confidence interval of 23.9 to 39.9 and a permutation p of 1e-4. The effect is real but bounded. By 300 actions that same linear model is worse than state alone, while the spread between tickets from one start state grows to 330 mm, so the influence persists even where this predictor stops being useful.

Perception Incremental LiDAR-Camera Sensor Fusion for Reactive Cone Following in the Formula Student Driverless Simulator
Daniel Ortiz Valencia, RAM Racing Driverless
Formula Student Driverless Simulator, 2026
Website/ PDF/ arXiv soon

A LiDAR-only controller completes laps but weaves on every straight, because it cannot tell blue cones from yellow. Fusing a YOLOv5 camera detector with LiDAR depth brings the two sensors into agreement at 6 cm average match accuracy and removes the oscillation.

Reinforcement Learning Reinforcement Learning for Steering Control in the Formula Student Driverless Simulator: Structural Failure Modes of Soft Actor-Critic and the Case for PPO
Daniel Ortiz Valencia
Electrical & Computer Engineering, Colorado State University, 2026
Website/ PDF

Six training runs of Soft Actor-Critic, all six of which collapsed. The failures trace to three structural properties of the algorithm rather than to tuning: replay-buffer poisoning, automatic entropy collapse, and a critic whose calibration is tied to one reward shape. PPO removes all three by construction.

Safety / ML YOLO Color Anomaly Detection for FSAE Driverless: From Ground-Truth Dataset Construction to an XGBoost Safety Gate
Daniel Ortiz Valencia
ECE553, Colorado State University, 2026
PDF/ code soon

YOLO fails silently, returning a confident colour label that happens to be wrong. I labelled 129,025 cone detections to measure how often, then trained an XGBoost gate that catches roughly 91% of those errors before they reach the steering controller, at under 0.3 ms per frame on a Raspberry Pi 4.

Teleoperation VR Teleoperation Kit for Robot Manipulators
Aurel Arnold, Daniel Ortiz Valencia, and contributors
Open source, integrates with LeRobot
GitHub

A WebXR page running in a Meta Quest streams 6-DoF controller poses over a WebSocket, and a Python teleoperator turns them into joint commands through differential inverse kinematics, with forward kinematics and Jacobians read from a MuJoCo model built from the arm's URDF. Clutch-relative mapping, a reach limit that feels like a wall through haptics, decoupled position and wrist IK with manipulability-adaptive damping, and optional WebRTC camera panels rendered inside VR. Drops into LeRobot as a Teleoperator.
